# Organic Post Templates

Use these for the **first three** organic posts that should be published on both the Facebook Page and the Instagram Business account before any ad goes live. They serve two purposes:

1. The profile doesn't look empty to a prospect who clicks through from an ad.
2. The accounts have real, original activity, which is good signal for Meta's review systems.

Replace the bracketed placeholders with the client's details from the intake.

---

## Post 1 — Intro post

**Goal:** introduce the business. Who, what, where, how to reach them.

**Media:** owner-led photo if available, otherwise the strongest finished-work photo.

**Caption template:**

```
Welcome to [Business Name].

We're a [years-in-business]-year [trade] business serving [service area]. We specialize in [primary service] and [secondary service].

If you're looking for [primary outcome the customer wants], we'd love to give you a free estimate.

📞 Call us: [phone]
🌐 [website]

[Three or four hashtags: #City #Service #Trade #LocalBusiness]
```

**Notes:**
- Pin this post to the top of the Facebook Page for the first 30 days.
- On Instagram, this becomes the first feed tile, so the image quality matters.

---

## Post 2 — Finished-work post

**Goal:** show what the work actually looks like. Build credibility.

**Media:** the single strongest finished-job photo, or a before/after pair. No stock images.

**Caption template:**

```
Just wrapped this [job type] in [town/neighborhood].

[One sentence describing what we did — material, scope, anything that signals quality.]

If your [thing the service applies to — driveway, roof, lawn, system] is due for [the service], we offer free estimates across [service area].

📞 [phone]
🌐 [website]

[Three or four hashtags]
```

**Notes:**
- This is the post most likely to get shared. Image quality decides whether it gets engagement.
- If permission to post the location was granted, tag the town for local reach.

---

## Post 3 — Owner / trust post

**Goal:** put a human face on the business and surface the trust signals.

**Media:** photo of the owner on a job, the branded truck/trailer with the crew, or a clean image of the license/insurance card (numbers blurred).

**Caption template:**

```
Meet [Owner Name], the owner of [Business Name].

[One or two sentences in the owner's voice — why they started, what they care about, how they treat customers.]

Licensed, insured, and [years]+ years on the tools. When you call [Business Name], you're getting [the owner] and a crew that treats your property like our own.

📞 [phone] — ask for [Owner first name].
🌐 [website]

[Three or four hashtags]
```

**Notes:**
- Don't fake the owner voice. If the owner won't write it themselves, interview them for 5 minutes and quote them.
- This post is the one to revisit as a paid creative later (after ad creative testing in week 2).

---

## Caption guidelines (apply to all three)

- Lead with a hook in the first line. The "see more" cut happens around line 2 on mobile.
- Use line breaks generously. Walls of text die.
- Phone number first, website second, in that order.
- 3–5 hashtags is the sweet spot for local service. More than 10 looks spammy.
- No emojis in the first line. One or two later in the post is fine.

## Posting cadence after launch

- Once a week minimum for the first 90 days.
- Mix: 1 finished-work post, 1 owner/trust or behind-the-scenes, 1 educational or seasonal tip per month.
- Repurpose the best organic post into an ad creative once it has any engagement signal.
